**ENGLISH:**

In the celestial dance of cosmos, a tale of deceit and longing unfurls, where the moonlit churning sea is both witness and stage. This is the story of "Rahu Aur Ketu," a legend painted in vibrant strokes akin to Mithila art—a tapestry where gods and demons contended for the nectar of immortality, Amrit, born of the cosmic ocean churning, or Samudra Manthan. Rahu, driven by insatiable desire, disguised himself among the divine, his heart fluttering with the unspoken wish to transgress celestial boundaries. For a fleeting moment, he tasted the ambrosial essence, stealing it from destiny itself.

But the cosmos is vigilant, and Vishnu, the eternal preserver, wielded his divine discus with a swift justice that cleft the night sky, severing Rahu's form. Yet in this celestial symphony, the severed head, Rahu, and the dismembered tail, Ketu, transcended physical bounds to become shadowy nodes in the astral dance—intangible yet powerful harbingers of fate and transformation. In Mithila’s scrolls, they are painted vivid against the tapestry of time, teaching of desires' impermanence and the cosmic order that governs the vaults of heaven.
